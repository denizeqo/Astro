{% trans "What Is Astrology?" %}

{% blocktrans %} Astrology is an ancient tradition based on the observation of the sky and celestial bodies. Throughout the centuries, humanity has observed the movement of Stars, Planets, and Constellations, seeking to understand their relationship with life on Earth. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans %} Astrology is not currently considered an official science by the scientific community. It is a traditional and symbolic discipline that has developed through centuries of observation and experience. Its language uses many elements also found in astronomy: Stars, Planets, Constellations, Houses, coordinates, and celestial cycles. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans %} In the astrological tradition, these elements are used to interpret personality, characteristics, potential, and cycles of life, beginning with the date and time of birth and subsequently observing Transits and Progressions. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans %} Throughout history, Astrology has also intersected with other traditional forms of knowledge, such as Numerology, Geometry, and Alchemy, creating an increasingly rich symbolic language. {% endblocktrans %}

{% trans "In Summary astrology observes the sky in search of meaning in life on Earth." %}

{% trans "The Zodiac and Precession" %}

{% blocktrans %} To understand Astrology, it is important to know a fundamental astronomical phenomenon: the Precession of the Equinoxes. Earth's axis slowly performs a circular movement through space. This produces a very slow displacement of the equinoctial point along the Zodiac. A complete precession cycle lasts about 26,000 years. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans %} The point we call 0° Aries therefore does not remain fixed relative to the stars. Over millennia it slowly moves through the constellations. The discovery of precession is traditionally attributed to Hipparchus, around the 2nd century BCE. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans %} This creates an important distinction between zodiac signs and constellations. The sign of Aries, for example, no longer exactly coincides with the constellation Aries. Today the equinoctial point associated with the beginning of Aries lies in the region of Pisces. {% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans %} From this comes the concept of Astrological Ages, determined by the slow movement of the equinoctial point through the constellations. According to one of the most widespread traditional divisions, we are in the concluding phase of the Age of Pisces and approaching the Age of Aquarius. The exact date is not universal because it depends on how constellation boundaries are defined. {% endblocktrans %}
